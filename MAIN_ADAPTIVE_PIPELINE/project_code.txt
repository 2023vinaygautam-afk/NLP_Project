COVID-19 Transmission and Community Prevention

COVID-19 is caused by infection with SARS-CoV-2. Transmission can occur when an infected person releases respiratory particles while breathing, speaking, coughing, or sneezing. Risk generally increases in crowded or poorly ventilated indoor environments and when people are in close proximity for prolonged periods. Prevention therefore combines several measures rather than relying on a single intervention.

Improving indoor ventilation and filtration can reduce the concentration of infectious particles in shared air. Staying home when acutely ill can reduce exposure to other people. Mask use can provide an additional layer of protection in settings where respiratory virus transmission is elevated or where vulnerable people are present. Hand hygiene remains useful for reducing transmission of many infections, although respiratory transmission is a major route for COVID-19.

Vaccination is an important prevention measure because it prepares the immune system to respond to the virus. Vaccine protection against infection can change over time and across variants, while protection against severe disease can remain stronger. Public-health programs therefore consider vaccination together with surveillance, clinical care, ventilation, and risk-based protective measures.

Community prevention works best when measures are matched to the local epidemiological situation. Public-health authorities may use indicators such as reported cases, hospitalizations, wastewater signals, and outbreak reports to guide communication and interventions. Individuals can reduce risk by considering the intensity of local transmission, their own health risks, and the vulnerability of people around them.

COVID-19 Vaccination: Basic Concepts

COVID-19 vaccines are designed to stimulate an immune response against SARS-CoV-2 without requiring a person to experience the disease itself. Different vaccine platforms can present viral information to the immune system in different ways. After vaccination, immune cells can recognize relevant viral components and mount a faster response when exposed later.

Vaccination does not guarantee that a person will never become infected. Vaccine effectiveness varies by outcome, product, population, time since vaccination, and circulating variant. Public-health evaluation commonly distinguishes protection against infection, symptomatic disease, hospitalization, and death. These outcomes should not be treated as interchangeable.

Immunity can change over time. Previous infection may also contribute to immune protection, producing what researchers often describe as hybrid immunity when vaccination and infection have both occurred. Because immunity and circulating viruses change, vaccination recommendations may be updated as evidence develops.

When communicating about vaccines, it is useful to distinguish relative risk, absolute risk, vaccine effectiveness, and adverse-event rates. A clear explanation should specify the population, comparison group, time period, and health outcome. This prevents misleading conclusions from a single percentage or isolated statistic.

Evaluating Vaccine Effectiveness

Vaccine effectiveness is usually estimated by comparing disease outcomes between vaccinated and comparison groups under real-world conditions. Observational studies may use cohort, case-control, or test-negative designs. Researchers must account for factors such as age, prior infection, underlying conditions, healthcare-seeking behavior, exposure patterns, and calendar period.

Randomized controlled trials can provide strong evidence about efficacy under defined study conditions, while observational studies are especially useful after vaccines are deployed at large scale. Real-world effectiveness may differ from trial efficacy because populations, exposure levels, circulating variants, and vaccination schedules differ.

A useful interpretation of vaccine effectiveness requires knowing the endpoint. For example, effectiveness against hospitalization is a different quantity from effectiveness against infection. Waning protection may also appear differently for different outcomes. Researchers may stratify results by age, time since vaccination, product, or variant period.

Confounding and selection bias are important limitations in observational vaccine studies. Statistical adjustment can reduce some biases, but it cannot guarantee that every relevant difference between groups has been measured. Results should therefore be interpreted in the context of study design, uncertainty intervals, sample size, and the population studied.

Incidence and Prevalence in Infectious Disease Epidemiology

Incidence and prevalence describe different aspects of disease occurrence. Incidence focuses on new cases occurring in a population during a specified period. Prevalence describes the proportion of a population that has a condition at a particular time or over a defined period.

For an infectious disease, incidence can help describe how quickly new infections are occurring. Prevalence can reflect both new cases and the duration of illness. A disease with short duration may have substantial incidence but relatively low point prevalence. Conversely, a chronic condition can have lower incidence but high prevalence because people remain affected for long periods.

Epidemiologists define the population, time interval, case definition, and measurement method carefully. Changes in testing availability or reporting practices can affect observed incidence without representing an equivalent change in true transmission. For COVID-19, widespread changes in testing behavior made reported case counts difficult to interpret in isolation during some periods.

Other indicators, including hospitalization, mortality, test positivity, syndromic surveillance, and wastewater measurements, can complement reported cases. Using multiple indicators provides a broader picture of disease activity.

Reproduction Numbers and Outbreak Growth

The basic reproduction number, R0, describes the average number of secondary infections generated by a typical infectious individual in a fully susceptible population under specified assumptions. It is a theoretical epidemiological quantity and should not be interpreted as a fixed property of a pathogen in every setting.

The effective reproduction number, often written Rt or Re, reflects transmission under current conditions. Vaccination, prior immunity, behavioral changes, public-health interventions, and population structure can all affect effective transmission. When the effective reproduction number is above one, infections can increase under the relevant conditions; when it remains below one, transmission tends to decline.

Estimating reproduction numbers requires assumptions and data. Delays between infection, symptom onset, testing, reporting, and hospitalization can affect estimates. Analysts may therefore use statistical models and nowcasting methods to account for reporting delays.

Reproduction numbers should be interpreted together with other indicators. A single estimate can be uncertain, especially when case counts are small or surveillance systems are changing. The same reproduction number can also correspond to different absolute levels of disease depending on the size and immunity of the population.

Contact Tracing and Exposure Management

Contact tracing is a public-health process used to identify people who may have been exposed to an infectious disease and provide them with relevant information and support. For respiratory infections, contact definitions may consider proximity, duration, setting, and timing relative to infectiousness.

A typical workflow includes identifying a confirmed or probable case, determining the period during which transmission could have occurred, interviewing the case when appropriate, identifying contacts, and communicating recommended actions. Digital tools can assist with notification or data management, but they do not replace public-health judgment.

Contact tracing is most effective when cases and contacts can be identified quickly and when people have access to testing, healthcare, isolation resources, and accurate information. Privacy and confidentiality are important because contact-tracing systems handle sensitive health and exposure information.

The effectiveness of contact tracing can decrease when community transmission is extremely high, when many infections are asymptomatic, or when cases cannot be identified promptly. In such situations, broad prevention measures and community surveillance may be more practical complements.

Wastewater Surveillance for Respiratory Viruses

Wastewater surveillance analyzes biological material in sewage to estimate the presence or trends of infectious agents within a community. Because infected individuals can shed viral genetic material into wastewater, wastewater measurements can provide population-level information that does not depend on every infected person seeking a clinical test.

Wastewater surveillance can be especially useful when individual testing declines. Trends may provide an early signal of changing transmission, although the relationship between wastewater concentration and the exact number of infections is not straightforward.

Interpretation requires attention to sampling location, population served by the treatment system, rainfall and dilution, laboratory methods, sampling frequency, and changes in shedding patterns. Wastewater data are most useful when combined with clinical surveillance, hospital data, and other epidemiological indicators.

Wastewater surveillance generally does not identify individual infected people. Its population-level nature can therefore complement other surveillance systems while reducing dependence on individual testing. It is a monitoring tool rather than a direct diagnostic test for an individual.

SARS-CoV-2 Variant Surveillance

Variant surveillance monitors genetic changes in SARS-CoV-2 and assesses whether emerging lineages have characteristics relevant to public health. Sequencing can identify viral lineages from selected clinical specimens, while genomic databases allow laboratories and researchers to compare sequences over time and across locations.

A newly detected mutation does not automatically mean that a variant is more transmissible or more severe. Researchers evaluate changes using laboratory studies, epidemiological data, clinical outcomes, and population-level observations. Multiple mutations can occur together, and the biological effect of a mutation depends on its location and context.

Variant surveillance helps public-health agencies detect changes that may affect diagnostics, therapeutics, vaccines, or transmission patterns. However, sequencing samples are not always representative of all infections. Differences in sampling and sequencing capacity can influence which variants are observed.

Genomic surveillance works best when linked with epidemiological and clinical information. Sequence data can identify what is circulating, while other datasets help determine the practical public-health significance of those lineages.

Infectious Disease Outbreak Investigation

An outbreak investigation seeks to determine whether an unusual increase in disease has occurred, characterize affected people and settings, identify likely sources or transmission routes, and guide control measures. The investigation begins with a clear case definition and confirmation that the observed increase is greater than expected.

Investigators construct descriptive summaries by time, place, and person. An epidemic curve can show the timing of illness onset and may provide clues about the type of exposure. Mapping cases can identify geographic clustering, while demographic analysis can reveal differences by age, occupation, or other characteristics.

Analytical epidemiology may compare exposures among cases and controls or among exposed and unexposed groups. Investigators must consider recall bias, selection bias, confounding, and incomplete reporting. Laboratory testing and genomic sequencing can provide additional evidence.

Control measures should not necessarily wait until every scientific question is resolved. Depending on the pathogen and circumstances, measures can include isolation, vaccination, environmental controls, communication, testing, and targeted restrictions. Investigators continue evaluating the outbreak as new evidence becomes available.

Public Health Surveillance Systems

Public health surveillance is the continuous and systematic collection, analysis, interpretation, and dissemination of health information for action. Surveillance systems can monitor infectious diseases through case reports, laboratory results, hospital records, mortality data, syndromic indicators, sentinel sites, and community-level measurements.

A surveillance system begins with a defined objective. The case definition determines which events are counted, while reporting rules specify how and when information is submitted. Data quality depends on completeness, timeliness, consistency, and representativeness.

Passive surveillance relies on routine reporting and can be relatively efficient but may miss cases. Active surveillance involves more direct efforts to identify cases and can improve completeness at greater cost. Sentinel surveillance collects detailed information from selected sites and may be useful for monitoring trends.

Surveillance data should be interpreted carefully. Changes in reporting systems, testing practices, healthcare access, or case definitions can create apparent changes that are not purely epidemiological. Public-health decision-making therefore benefits from triangulating multiple data sources.

Infection Prevention in Healthcare Settings

Healthcare facilities require layered infection-prevention measures because patients and healthcare workers may encounter infectious individuals in close-contact environments. Core practices include hand hygiene, appropriate use of personal protective equipment, environmental cleaning, respiratory hygiene, and safe patient placement.

Administrative measures can include screening or triage procedures, staff training, vaccination programs, and policies for managing symptomatic healthcare workers. Engineering controls include ventilation, physical separation, and room design. Personal protective equipment is selected according to the anticipated exposure and task.

Infection prevention programs also depend on monitoring. Facilities can track healthcare-associated infections, respiratory illness among staff, compliance with selected practices, and environmental indicators. Training should be repeated and adapted when evidence or recommendations change.

No single control is sufficient in every situation. Layered prevention reduces reliance on one barrier and can be adapted according to the pathogen, transmission route, patient population, and local outbreak conditions.

Vaccine Safety and Adverse Event Monitoring

Vaccine safety monitoring continues after vaccines are authorized or licensed because very rare adverse events may only become detectable when millions of doses are administered. Surveillance systems collect reports of health events that occur after vaccination and investigate signals that may indicate a potential association.

An adverse event following immunization is not automatically caused by the vaccine. The event may be coincidental, associated with another medical condition, or potentially related to vaccination. Safety assessment therefore considers background rates, timing, biological plausibility, comparison groups, and other evidence.

Passive reporting systems are valuable for signal detection but can be affected by underreporting, stimulated reporting, and incomplete clinical information. Active surveillance and epidemiological studies can provide stronger estimates of incidence and relative risk.

Clear communication should distinguish a reported event from a confirmed causal relationship. Vaccine safety decisions are based on accumulated evidence rather than a single report. Monitoring can lead to updated warnings, recommendations, or continued use when evidence supports a favorable benefit-risk profile.

Risk Communication During Infectious Disease Events

Risk communication provides people with information needed to understand a public-health threat and make informed decisions. Effective communication should explain what is known, what is uncertain, what actions are recommended, and why recommendations may change.

Numbers should be presented with context. For example, a percentage without a denominator, comparison group, time period, or outcome can be misleading. Communicators should distinguish absolute risk from relative risk and avoid implying certainty when evidence remains limited.

Trust can be supported by acknowledging uncertainty rather than hiding it. During a rapidly changing outbreak, evidence can evolve as new studies become available. Explaining the reason for an updated recommendation can help people understand that changing guidance does not necessarily mean that earlier information was intentionally misleading.

Communication should also consider language, accessibility, cultural context, and misinformation. Public-health messages are more useful when they provide practical actions and identify reliable sources for additional information.

Respiratory Infection Prevention in Schools

Schools are environments where students and staff interact frequently, so respiratory infections can spread through close contact and shared indoor air. Prevention strategies should be proportionate to local disease activity and the needs of the school community.

Ventilation can reduce accumulation of infectious respiratory particles indoors. Outdoor activities can provide lower-risk alternatives when practical. Encouraging people with acute respiratory illness to stay home can reduce opportunities for transmission.

Schools can also support routine vaccination according to national and local recommendations. Clear procedures for responding to clusters of illness help staff identify when public-health consultation may be needed. Communication with families should explain symptoms, prevention measures, and when students should remain at home.

Outbreak response should consider educational continuity as well as disease control. Measures can be targeted according to evidence rather than relying automatically on broad closures. Coordination among school administrators, healthcare professionals, families, and public-health authorities is important.

Protecting Higher-Risk Populations During Respiratory Virus Activity

Some people have a higher risk of severe outcomes from respiratory infections because of age, underlying medical conditions, immune status, pregnancy, or other factors. Public-health planning can reduce harm by ensuring that higher-risk groups have access to vaccination, timely testing when appropriate, clinical care, and clear prevention information.

Protection can involve individual and community measures. Healthcare facilities and residential settings may use additional precautions during periods of elevated transmission. Family members and caregivers can reduce exposure opportunities when respiratory viruses are circulating widely.

Risk is not determined by a single characteristic. Age, vaccination status, previous immunity, treatment access, and circulating viral strains can interact. Public-health communication should therefore avoid assuming that all members of a broad category have identical risk.

Equitable access is an important part of prevention. Barriers such as transportation, cost, language, digital access, and healthcare availability can affect whether people are able to use recommended services.

Testing and Antiviral Treatment in COVID-19 Care

Testing can help identify SARS-CoV-2 infection and inform clinical or public-health decisions. Different tests have different characteristics. Molecular tests can detect viral genetic material, while antigen tests detect viral proteins and may provide rapid results. Test performance depends on timing, specimen collection, and the stage of infection.

A negative result does not always exclude infection, particularly when testing occurs early or when the test has lower sensitivity. Interpretation should consider symptoms, exposure history, timing, and the specific test used.

Antiviral treatment is intended to reduce the risk of severe disease in eligible patients. Eligibility, timing, contraindications, drug interactions, and treatment selection require clinical assessment. Public-health information should not replace individualized medical advice.

Testing and treatment programs are most effective when people can access testing promptly and healthcare systems can evaluate eligible patients without unnecessary delay. Surveillance data can also help authorities understand changes in testing demand and disease activity.

Health Literacy and Infectious Disease Misinformation

Health literacy involves the ability to find, understand, evaluate, and use health information. During infectious disease outbreaks, people may encounter rapidly changing information through news, social media, messaging applications, and informal networks.

Misinformation can arise from incorrect information, misunderstanding, outdated evidence, or misleading presentation of accurate facts. A useful evaluation process checks the source, date, evidence, population studied, and whether the claim matches the underlying data.

Health communication should avoid presenting preliminary findings as settled conclusions. A single laboratory experiment or small observational study may not answer a population-level question. Stronger conclusions generally require evidence from multiple complementary studies.

Practical health literacy strategies include checking information against recognized public-health or medical sources, looking for the original study when possible, and distinguishing a reported association from proof of causation. Clear explanations can reduce confusion without assuming that every person who has encountered inaccurate information is acting in bad faith.

Global Outbreak Preparedness and Response

Outbreak preparedness involves building the capacity to detect, assess, communicate about, and respond to infectious disease threats before they become large emergencies. Preparedness includes laboratory capacity, surveillance systems, trained personnel, medical supplies, vaccination infrastructure, data systems, and emergency coordination mechanisms.

Early detection depends on timely information. Countries and institutions may use laboratory networks, clinical surveillance, event-based reporting, genomic sequencing, and cross-border information sharing. A signal does not always represent a major outbreak, but rapid assessment can determine whether further investigation is required.

Response planning includes clear roles and responsibilities. Hospitals may need surge plans, while public-health agencies may require mechanisms for contact tracing, risk communication, and resource allocation. Exercises and simulations can identify weaknesses before a real emergency occurs.

Preparedness is an ongoing process. After an outbreak, evaluations can identify which systems worked, which failed, and which improvements should be institutionalized. Investments in routine public-health capacity can strengthen response to multiple infectious threats rather than a single pathogen.

Epidemiological Models for Infectious Disease Planning

Mathematical and computational models can help researchers explore how infectious diseases may spread under different assumptions. Compartmental models such as SIR divide populations into categories such as susceptible, infected, and recovered. More detailed models can add age groups, vaccination status, hospitalization, geographic structure, or other characteristics.

Models are not crystal balls. Their outputs depend on assumptions, parameter estimates, data quality, and the scenario being examined. A model can be useful even when its exact numerical forecast is uncertain because it can show how outcomes change when assumptions change.

Model calibration uses observed data to estimate parameters or evaluate whether simulated trajectories are consistent with historical patterns. Validation and sensitivity analysis can identify which assumptions have the greatest effect on results.

Public-health decision-makers should interpret model outputs alongside surveillance data and expert assessment. Scenario projections are particularly sensitive to changes in behavior, immunity, viral characteristics, interventions, and healthcare capacity. Transparent reporting of assumptions and uncertainty is therefore essential.

Evaluating Public Health Responses After an Outbreak

After an infectious disease outbreak, a structured evaluation can examine what happened, which interventions were implemented, how quickly systems responded, and what improvements are needed. Evaluation should consider epidemiological outcomes as well as operational, social, and economic consequences.

Useful indicators may include time from signal detection to investigation, testing turnaround time, vaccination coverage, hospitalization trends, healthcare capacity, communication reach, and the timeliness of public-health reporting. The exact measures depend on the outbreak and the objectives of the response.

Evaluation should distinguish correlation from causation. A decline in cases after an intervention does not automatically prove that the intervention caused the decline because transmission can also change due to immunity, behavior, seasonality, or other factors.

After-action reviews can translate findings into concrete improvements. These may include updating response plans, strengthening laboratory networks, improving data interoperability, expanding workforce training, or revising communication procedures. The goal is to convert experience into preparedness for future infectious disease events.

# 2. FIVE EVALUATION QUERIES AND RELEVANCE LABELS

RELEVANCE = {

# =====================================================
# 3. DOCUMENT ID NORMALIZATION
# =====================================================

def normalize_document_id(value):

    value = str(value).strip()

    value = value.replace(".txt", "")

    if value.startswith("D") and len(value) == 3:
        return value

    for number in range(1, 21):

        if value.startswith(f"{number:02d}_"):
            return f"D{number:02d}"

    return value


def normalize_documents(value):

    if value is None:
        return []

    if isinstance(value, float) and pd.isna(value):
        return []

    if isinstance(value, str):

        value = value.strip()

        if not value:
            return []

        try:
            parsed = json.loads(value)

            if isinstance(parsed, list):
                value = parsed

        except json.JSONDecodeError:

            value = value.replace(";", ",")

            value = [
                item.strip()
                for item in value.split(",")
                if item.strip()
            ]

    if isinstance(value, dict):
        value = list(value.keys())

    if not isinstance(value, (list, tuple, set)):
        value = [value]

    return list(dict.fromkeys(
        normalize_document_id(item)
        for item in value
        if str(item).strip()
    ))


# =====================================================
# 4. METRIC CALCULATION
# =====================================================

def calculate_metrics(retrieved, relevant, k=5):

    retrieved = normalize_documents(retrieved)

    retrieved_set = set(retrieved)
    relevant_set = set(relevant)

    true_positives = retrieved_set & relevant_set

    false_positives = retrieved_set - relevant_set

    false_negatives = relevant_set - retrieved_set

    tp = len(true_positives)
    fp = len(false_positives)
    fn = len(false_negatives)

    precision = (
        tp / (tp + fp)
        if tp + fp > 0
        else 0.0
    )

    recall = (
        tp / (tp + fn)
        if tp + fn > 0
        else 0.0
    )

    f1 = (
        2 * precision * recall / (precision + recall)
        if precision + recall > 0
        else 0.0
    )

    top_k = retrieved[:k]

    relevant_top_k = sum(
        doc in relevant_set
        for doc in top_k
    )

    precision_at_k = relevant_top_k / k

    recall_at_k = (
        relevant_top_k / len(relevant_set)
        if relevant_set
        else 0.0
    )

    return {
        "TP": tp,
        "FP": fp,
        "FN": fn,
        "Precision": precision,
        "Recall": recall,
        "F1_Score": f1,
        "Precision@5": precision_at_k,
        "Recall@5": recall_at_k,
        "Retrieved_Count": len(retrieved)
    }


# =====================================================
# 5. LOAD RETRIEVAL RESULTS
# =====================================================

def load_results(file_path):

    if not file_path.exists():

        print(f"\nFile not found: {file_path}")

        return {}

    with open(file_path, "r", encoding="utf-8") as file:

        data = json.load(file)

    if isinstance(data, dict):

        if isinstance(data.get("results"), list):
            data = data["results"]

        elif isinstance(data.get("retrieval_results"), list):
            data = data["retrieval_results"]

        elif isinstance(data.get("queries"), list):
            data = data["queries"]

        else:
            data = [
                {"query": key, **value}
                for key, value in data.items()
                if isinstance(value, dict)
            ]

    result_map = {}

    for item in data:

        if not isinstance(item, dict):
            continue

        query = (
            item.get("query")
            or item.get("Query")
            or item.get("user_query")
        )

        if not query:
            continue

        retrieved = (
            item.get("retrieved_documents")
            or item.get("Retrieved_Documents")
            or item.get("retrieved")
            or item.get("results")
            or []
        )

        result_map[str(query).strip()] = normalize_documents(
            retrieved
        )

    return result_map


# =====================================================
# 6. EVALUATE ONE PIPELINE
# =====================================================

def evaluate_pipeline(result_map, pipeline_name):

    rows = []

    for query_id, (query, relevant) in enumerate(
        RELEVANCE.items(), start=1
    ):

        retrieved = result_map.get(query, [])

        metrics = calculate_metrics(
            retrieved,
            relevant,
            k=5
        )

        rows.append({

            "Query_ID": f"Q{query_id}",

            "Query": query,

            "Pipeline": pipeline_name,

            "Retrieved_Documents": "; ".join(retrieved),

            "Expected_Relevant_Documents": "; ".join(
                sorted(relevant)
            ),

            "Relevant_Count": len(relevant),

            **metrics

        })

    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


# =====================================================
# 7. MAIN COMPARATIVE EVALUATION
# =====================================================

def main():

    print("\nMAIN ADAPTIVE PIPELINE")
    print("=" * 65)

    print("SAME-QUERY BASELINE VS ADAPTIVE EVALUATION")

    baseline_map = load_results(RETRIEVAL_FILE)

    adaptive_map = load_results(ADAPTIVE_FILE)

    print("\nQueries defined:", len(RELEVANCE))

    print("Baseline query results:", len(baseline_map))

    print("Adaptive query results:", len(adaptive_map))

    baseline = evaluate_pipeline(
        baseline_map,
        "BASELINE"
    )

    adaptive = evaluate_pipeline(
        adaptive_map,
        "ADAPTIVE"
    )

    # -------------------------------------------------
    # 8. MERGE SAME QUERY RESULTS
    # -------------------------------------------------

    comparison = baseline.merge(
        adaptive,
        on=["Query_ID", "Query", "Relevant_Count"],
        suffixes=("_Baseline", "_Adaptive")
    )

    metric_names = [
        "Precision",
        "Recall",
        "F1_Score",
        "Precision@5",
        "Recall@5"

    summary = {}

def tokenize(text):

    return re.findall(
        r"\b[a-zA-Z0-9]+(?:[-'][a-zA-Z0-9]+)*\b",
        text.lower()
    )


# ============================================================
# STOPWORDS
# ============================================================

STOPWORDS = {
    "a", "an", "the", "is", "are", "was", "were",
    "be", "been", "being", "of", "in", "on", "at",
    "to", "for", "from", "with", "by", "as", "and",
    "or", "but", "this", "that", "these", "those",
    "it", "its", "they", "them", "their", "we",
    "our", "you", "your", "has", "have", "had"
}

# Preserve negation and clinically meaningful words.
STOPWORDS -= {
    "no", "not", "nor", "without"
}


# ============================================================
# LOAD CORPUS
# ============================================================

def load_corpus():

    path = RESULTS_DIR / "cleaned_corpus.json"

    with open(path, "r", encoding="utf-8") as file:
        data = json.load(file)

    documents = (
        data.get("documents", [])
        if isinstance(data, dict)
        else data
    )

    normalized = []

    for i, doc in enumerate(documents, start=1):

        doc_id = (
            doc.get("doc_id")
            or doc.get("id")
            or doc.get("document_id")
            or f"D{i:02d}"
        )

        text = (
            doc.get("cleaned_text")
            or doc.get("cleaned")
            or doc.get("text")
            or doc.get("content")
            or ""
        )

        normalized.append({
            "doc_id": str(doc_id),
            "text": str(text)
        })

    return normalized


# ============================================================
# PREPROCESSING PIPELINES
# ============================================================

def pipeline_a(text):

    """
    Pipeline A:
    Lowercase -> Tokenize -> Stopword Removal -> Stemming
    """

    tokens = tokenize(text)

    tokens = [
        token for token in tokens
        if token not in STOPWORDS
    ]

    stemmer = PorterStemmer()

    return [
        stemmer.stem(token)
        for token in tokens
    ]


def pipeline_b(text):

    """
    Pipeline B:
    Lowercase -> Stopword Removal -> Tokenize -> Lemmatization

    Stopword removal is applied to normalized whitespace-separated
    terms before the final tokenization step.
    """

    lowered = text.lower()

    words = lowered.split()

    filtered_text = " ".join(
        word for word in words
        if word.strip(".,;:!?()[]{}\"'") not in STOPWORDS
    )

    tokens = tokenize(filtered_text)

    lemmatizer = WordNetLemmatizer()

    return [
        lemmatizer.lemmatize(token)
        for token in tokens
    ]


# ============================================================
# BUILD INDEX
# ============================================================

def build_index(corpus, pipeline_function):

    index = defaultdict(set)

    for doc in corpus:

        tokens = pipeline_function(doc["text"])

        for token in set(tokens):

            index[token].add(doc["doc_id"])

    return dict(index)


# ============================================================
# LOAD QUERIES
# ============================================================

def load_queries():

    df = pd.read_csv(QUERY_FILE)

    df = df.dropna(
        subset=["query", "expected_document"]
    ).copy()

    df["query"] = df["query"].astype(str)

    df["expected_document"] = (
        df["expected_document"].astype(str)
    )

    return df


# ============================================================
# RETRIEVAL
# ============================================================

def retrieve_keyword(query, index, pipeline_function):

    query_tokens = pipeline_function(query)

    results = set()

    for token in query_tokens:

        results.update(
            index.get(token, set())
        )

    return results


# ============================================================
# METRICS
# ============================================================

def calculate_metrics(retrieved, relevant):

    retrieved = set(retrieved)

    relevant = set(relevant)

    tp = len(retrieved & relevant)

    precision = (
        tp / len(retrieved)
        if retrieved else 0.0
    )

    recall = (
        tp / len(relevant)
        if relevant else 0.0
    )

    f1 = (
        2 * precision * recall / (precision + recall)
        if precision + recall else 0.0
    )

    return {
        "precision": precision,
        "recall": recall,
        "f1": f1
    }

def select_retrieval_method(query_info):
    """
    Select a lexical retrieval strategy using query structure,
    extracted keywords, domain terms, and complexity.
    """

    query_type = query_info["query_type"]

    terms = query_info["terms"]

    keywords = query_info.get("keywords", terms)

    domain_terms = query_info.get("domain_terms", [])

    complexity = query_info["complexity"]

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # EXPLICIT RETRIEVAL STRATEGY
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    if query_type == "PHRASE":

        method = "PHRASE"

        reason = (
            "Quoted phrase syntax was detected. "
            "Phrase retrieval will be selected."
        )

        confidence = 1.0

    elif query_type == "NOT":

        method = "NOT"

        reason = (
            "An explicit NOT operator was detected. "
            "Exclusion-based retrieval will be selected."
        )

        confidence = 1.0

    elif query_type == "AND":

        method = "AND"

        reason = (
            "An explicit AND operator was detected. "
            "Intersection-based retrieval will be selected."
        )

        confidence = 1.0

    elif query_type == "OR":

        method = "OR"

        reason = (
            "An explicit OR operator was detected. "
            "Union-based retrieval will be selected."
        )

        confidence = 1.0

    else:

        method = "KEYWORD"

        if domain_terms:

            reason = (
                "The query contains domain-specific terms "
                "without explicit Boolean syntax. "
                "Keyword retrieval will be selected."
            )

        else:

            reason = (
                "No explicit Boolean or quoted phrase syntax "
                "was detected. Keyword retrieval will be selected."
            )

        confidence = 0.85

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # QUERY ANALYSIS
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    if complexity == "HIGH":

        complexity_reason = (
            "The query contains several terms or explicit "
            "Boolean structure."
        )

    elif complexity == "MEDIUM":

        complexity_reason = (
            "The query contains a moderate number of "
            "meaningful terms."
        )

    else:

        complexity_reason = (
            "The query contains a small number of meaningful terms."
        )

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # DECISION OUTPUT
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    return {
        "selected_method": method,
        "decision_reason": reason,
        "complexity_reason": complexity_reason,
        "query_complexity": complexity,
        "number_of_terms": len(terms),
        "number_of_keywords": len(keywords),
        "number_of_domain_terms": len(domain_terms),
        "decision_confidence": confidence
    }


# ============================================================
# ADAPTIVE DECISION FUNCTION
# ============================================================

def adaptive_decision(query):

    query_info = understand_query(query)

    decision = select_retrieval_method(query_info)

    return {
        **query_info,
        **decision
    }

# ==================================================
# MAIN ADAPTIVE PIPELINE
# ==================================================

def run_adaptive_pipeline(
    query,
    index,
    document_mapping,
    corpus
):

    # STEP 1: Understand the query

    query_info = understand_query(query)

    # STEP 2: Check domain relevance

    if not is_domain_relevant(query_info):

        return create_out_of_domain_response(
            query,
            query_info
        )

    # STEP 3: Select retrieval method

    decision = select_retrieval_method(query_info)

    retrieval_method = decision.get(
        "method",
        "KEYWORD"
    )

    # STEP 4: Execute selected lexical retrieval

    retrieval_output = retrieve_detailed(
        query,
        retrieval_method,
        index,
        corpus
    )

    # STEP 5: Enrich results

    enriched_results = []

    for result in retrieval_output.get("results", []):

        doc_id = result.get("document_id")

        document_name = document_mapping.get(
            doc_id,
            "Unknown document"
        )

        enriched_results.append({

            "document_id": doc_id,

            "document_name": document_name,

            "score": result.get("score", 0),

            "matched_terms": result.get(
                "matched_terms",
                []
            ),

            "coverage": result.get(
                "coverage",
                0
            ),

            "total_term_frequency": result.get(
                "total_term_frequency",
                0
            )

        })

    # STEP 6: Retrieval status

    if not enriched_results:

        status = "NO_MATCHING_EVIDENCE"

        reason = (
            "The query is within the supported domain, "
            "but no documents matched the selected "
            "lexical retrieval strategy."
        )

    else:

        status = "RETRIEVAL_COMPLETED"

        reason = (
            "Documents were retrieved using the "
            "selected lexical retrieval strategy."
        )

    # STEP 7: Final output

    final_output = {

        "query": query,

        "query_understanding": query_info,

        "adaptive_decision": {

            "method": retrieval_method,

            "confidence": decision.get(
                "confidence",
                0
            ),

            "reason": decision.get(
                "reason",
                reason
            )

        },

        "retrieval": {

            "method": retrieval_method,

            "count": len(enriched_results),

            "results": enriched_results

        },

        "status": status

    }

    return final_output

def normalize_corpus(corpus):

# ==================================================
# 4. TRAIN BPE TOKENIZER
# ==================================================

def train_bpe(corpus):

    tokenizer = Tokenizer(
        BPE(unk_token="[UNK]")
    )

    tokenizer.pre_tokenizer = Whitespace()

    trainer = BpeTrainer(

        vocab_size=500,

        min_frequency=2,

        special_tokens=[
            "[UNK]",
            "[PAD]",
            "[CLS]",
            "[SEP]",
            "[MASK]"
        ]

    )

    training_texts = [
        document["text"]
        for document in corpus
    ]

    tokenizer.train_from_iterator(
        training_texts,
        trainer=trainer
    )

    tokenizer.save(str(MODEL_FILE))

    return tokenizer


# ==================================================
# 5. ANALYZE BPE
# ==================================================

def analyze_bpe(tokenizer, text):

    encoding = tokenizer.encode(text)

    return {

        "tokens": encoding.tokens,

        "token_ids": encoding.ids,

        "token_count": len(encoding.tokens)

    }

        else:

            tag = "GENERAL"

        results.append({

            "pos": tag,

        })

# 2. TEXT STATISTICS
# ==================================================

def calculate_document_statistics(text):

    words = re.findall(r"\b\w+\b", text)

    sentences = re.split(r"[.!?]+", text)

    sentences = [
        sentence.strip()
        for sentence in sentences
        if sentence.strip()
    ]

    return {
        "character_count": len(text),
        "word_count": len(words),
        "sentence_count": len(sentences)
    }


# ==================================================
# 3. LOAD DOCUMENTS
# ==================================================

def load_documents():

    if not DOCUMENT_DIR.exists():
        raise FileNotFoundError(
            f"Document directory not found: {DOCUMENT_DIR}"
        )

    files = sorted(
        DOCUMENT_DIR.glob("*.txt")
    )

    if not files:
        raise FileNotFoundError(
            "No TXT documents found."
        )

    documents = []
    empty_documents = []

    for index, file_path in enumerate(files, start=1):

        document_id = f"D{index:02d}"

        text = file_path.read_text(
            encoding="utf-8",
            errors="replace"
        ).strip()

        if not text:
            empty_documents.append(file_path.name)
            print(f"WARNING: Empty document: {file_path.name}")
            continue

        stats = calculate_document_statistics(text)

        document = {
            "document_id": document_id,
            "file_name": file_path.name,
            "document_name": file_path.stem,
            "text": text,
            **stats
        }

        documents.append(document)

    if not documents:
        raise ValueError("No valid documents were loaded.")

    return documents, empty_documents


# ==================================================
# 4. CORPUS STATISTICS
# ==================================================

def calculate_corpus_statistics(documents):

    all_text = " ".join(
        doc["text"] for doc in documents
    )

    all_words = re.findall(
        r"\b\w+\b",
        all_text.lower()
    )

    vocabulary = set(all_words)

    total_documents = len(documents)

    total_words = len(all_words)

    total_sentences = sum(
        doc["sentence_count"] for doc in documents
    )

    total_characters = sum(
        doc["character_count"] for doc in documents
    )

    statistics = {
        "total_documents": total_documents,
        "total_sentences": total_sentences,
        "total_tokens": total_words,
        "total_characters": total_characters,
        "vocabulary_size": len(vocabulary),
        "average_document_length": round(
            total_words / total_documents, 2
        )
    }

    return statistics

def select_retrieval_method(query_info):
    """
    Select a lexical retrieval method using:

    1. Query type
    2. Query complexity
    3. Number of meaningful terms
    4. Domain-specific terms
    5. Query intent

    Supported methods:
    KEYWORD, PHRASE, AND, OR, NOT
    """

    query_type = str(
        query_info.get("query_type", "KEYWORD")
    ).upper()

    terms = query_info.get("terms", [])

    keywords = query_info.get("keywords", terms)

    domain_terms = query_info.get("domain_terms", [])

    complexity = str(
        query_info.get("complexity", "LOW")
    ).upper()

    query = str(query_info.get("query", ""))

    number_of_terms = len(terms)

    number_of_keywords = len(keywords)

    number_of_domain_terms = len(domain_terms)

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # STEP 1: SELECT RETRIEVAL METHOD
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    if query_type == "PHRASE":

        method = "PHRASE"

        reason = (
            "The query contains an explicitly quoted phrase. "
            "Phrase retrieval is selected to preserve term order."
        )

        confidence = 1.0

    elif query_type == "NOT":

        method = "NOT"

        reason = (
            "The query contains an explicit NOT operator. "
            "Exclusion-based lexical retrieval is selected."
        )

        confidence = 1.0

    elif query_type == "AND":

        method = "AND"

        reason = (
            "The query contains an explicit AND operator. "
            "Intersection-based lexical retrieval is selected."
        )

        confidence = 1.0

    elif query_type == "OR":

        method = "OR"

        reason = (
            "The query contains an explicit OR operator. "
            "Union-based lexical retrieval is selected."
        )

        confidence = 1.0

    else:

        method = "KEYWORD"

        if number_of_domain_terms > 0:

            reason = (
                "The query contains healthcare or public-health "
                "domain terms without explicit Boolean syntax. "
                "Keyword retrieval is selected."
            )

        else:

            reason = (
                "No explicit Boolean operator or quoted phrase "
                "was detected. Keyword retrieval is selected."
            )

        confidence = 0.85

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # STEP 2: ANALYZE QUERY COMPLEXITY
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    if complexity == "HIGH":

        complexity_reason = (
            "The query has high complexity based on the "
            "query-understanding module."
        )

    elif complexity == "MEDIUM":

        complexity_reason = (
            "The query has moderate complexity and contains "
            "multiple meaningful terms."
        )

    else:

        complexity_reason = (
            "The query has relatively low complexity."
        )

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # STEP 3: DETERMINE RETRIEVAL SCOPE
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    if method in ["AND", "PHRASE"]:

        retrieval_scope = "RESTRICTIVE"

        scope_reason = (
            "The retrieval method applies explicit term "
            "constraints or phrase matching."
        )

    elif method == "OR":

        retrieval_scope = "BROAD"

        scope_reason = (
            "OR retrieval allows documents matching any "
            "specified alternative."
        )

    elif method == "NOT":

        retrieval_scope = "EXCLUSION_BASED"

        scope_reason = (
            "The retrieval method excludes documents "
            "matching the prohibited term."
        )

    else:

        if number_of_keywords >= 5:

            retrieval_scope = "CONTROLLED"

            scope_reason = (
                "The query contains several keywords. "
                "Relevance ranking should prioritize documents "
                "covering more important query terms."
            )

        else:

            retrieval_scope = "STANDARD"

            scope_reason = (
                "Standard keyword retrieval is suitable "
                "for the detected query structure."
            )

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # STEP 4: DETERMINE EVIDENCE REQUIREMENT
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    if complexity == "HIGH" or number_of_keywords >= 5:

        evidence_requirement = "HIGH"

    elif number_of_keywords >= 3:

        evidence_requirement = "MEDIUM"

    else:

        evidence_requirement = "LOW"

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # STEP 5: DETERMINE INITIAL ACTION
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    if number_of_keywords == 0:

        initial_action = "REFINE"

        action_reason = (
            "No meaningful keywords were identified. "
            "The query should be refined before retrieval."
        )

    elif method in ["AND", "PHRASE"]:

        initial_action = "RETRIEVE"

        action_reason = (
            "The query contains explicit retrieval constraints "
            "that can be applied directly."
        )

    else:

        initial_action = "RETRIEVE"

        action_reason = (
            "Meaningful query terms are available for "
            "lexical retrieval."
        )

    # --------------------------------------------------------
    # STEP 6: QUERY ANALYSIS SUMMARY
    # --------------------------------------------------------

    return {
        "selected_method": method,

def load_corpus():
    with open(INPUT_FILE, "r", encoding="utf-8") as file:
        data = json.load(file)

    if isinstance(data, dict):
        documents = data.get("documents", [])
    else:
        documents = data

    normalized = []

    for i, doc in enumerate(documents, start=1):
        doc_id = (
            doc.get("doc_id")
            or doc.get("id")
            or doc.get("document_id")
            or f"D{i:02d}"
        )

        text = (
            doc.get("cleaned_text")
            or doc.get("cleaned")
            or doc.get("text")
            or doc.get("content")
            or ""
        )

        name = (
            doc.get("document_name")
            or doc.get("name")
            or doc.get("filename")
            or doc_id
        )

        normalized.append({
            "doc_id": str(doc_id),
            "name": str(name),
            "text": str(text)
        })

    return normalized


def tokenize(text):
    return re.findall(r"\b[a-zA-Z0-9]+(?:[-'][a-zA-Z0-9]+)*\b", text.lower())


def build_index(documents):
    inverted_index = defaultdict(dict)
    document_lengths = {}

    for doc in documents:
        doc_id = doc["doc_id"]
        tokens = tokenize(doc["text"])

        document_lengths[doc_id] = len(tokens)

        frequencies = Counter(tokens)

        for term, frequency in frequencies.items():
            inverted_index[term][doc_id] = frequency

    return dict(inverted_index), document_lengths


def calculate_statistics(index, documents, document_lengths):
    total_terms = sum(document_lengths.values())

    statistics = {
        "total_documents": len(documents),
        "unique_terms": len(index),
        "total_token_occurrences": total_terms,
        "average_document_length": (
            round(total_terms / len(documents), 2)
            if documents else 0
        ),
        "average_document_frequency": (
            round(
                sum(len(postings) for postings in index.values())
                / len(index),
                2
            )
            if index else 0
        ),
        "top_20_terms_by_document_frequency": sorted(
            [
                {
                    "term": term,
                    "document_frequency": len(postings)
                }
                for term, postings in index.items()
            ],
            key=lambda item: (
                -item["document_frequency"],
                item["term"]
            )
        )[:20],
        "document_lengths": document_lengths
    }

    return statistics

def get_wordnet_pos(tag):

    if tag.startswith("J"):
        return wordnet.ADJ

    elif tag.startswith("V"):
        return wordnet.VERB

    elif tag.startswith("N"):
        return wordnet.NOUN

    elif tag.startswith("R"):
        return wordnet.ADV

    return wordnet.NOUN


# ==================================================

# ==================================================

def nltk_lemmatize(tokens):

    tagged_tokens = nltk.pos_tag(tokens)

    results = []

    for word, tag in tagged_tokens:

        pos = get_wordnet_pos(tag)

        lemma = wordnet_lemmatizer.lemmatize(
            word,
            pos=pos
        )

        results.append(lemma)

    return results


# ==================================================
# 6. SPACY LEMMATIZATION
# ==================================================

def spacy_lemmatize(text):

    doc = nlp(text)

    return [
        token.lemma_.lower()
        for token in doc
        if not token.is_space
    ]

def process_corpus(documents):

    methods = {
        "NLTK": nltk_lemmatize,
        "Custom": custom_lemmatize
    }

    summary = {}
    document_results = []
    word_examples = {}

    for method_name, method in methods.items():

        all_lemmas = []

        for document in documents:

            tokens = tokenize(
                document["cleaned_text"]
            )

            lemmas = method(tokens)

            all_lemmas.extend(lemmas)

            document_results.append({

                "document_id": document["document_id"],
                "method": method_name,
                "token_count": len(lemmas),
                "vocabulary_size": len(set(lemmas)),
                "lemmatized_tokens": lemmas
            })

            for original, lemma in zip(tokens, lemmas):

                if original != lemma:

                    word_examples.setdefault(
                        original, {}
                    )

                    word_examples[original][method_name] = lemma

        summary[method_name] = {

            "total_tokens": len(all_lemmas),

            "vocabulary_size": len(set(all_lemmas)),

            "average_tokens_per_document": round(
                len(all_lemmas) / len(documents), 2
            ) if documents else 0
        }

    # Process spaCy separately because it uses full text
    all_spacy_lemmas = []

    for document in documents:

        lemmas = spacy_lemmatize(
            document["cleaned_text"]
        )

        all_spacy_lemmas.extend(lemmas)

        document_results.append({

            "document_id": document["document_id"],
            "method": "spaCy",
            "token_count": len(lemmas),
            "vocabulary_size": len(set(lemmas)),
            "lemmatized_tokens": lemmas
        })

    summary["spaCy"] = {

        "total_tokens": len(all_spacy_lemmas),

        "vocabulary_size": len(set(all_spacy_lemmas)),

        "average_tokens_per_document": round(
            len(all_spacy_lemmas) / len(documents), 2
        ) if documents else 0
    }

    return summary, document_results, word_examples

        "spacy_only": [

            for item in sorted(spacy_set - custom_set)
        ],

        "custom_only": [

            for item in sorted(custom_set - spacy_set)

def tokenize_text(text):

    pattern = (
        r"\b[A-Za-z]+(?:[-'][A-Za-z0-9]+)*\b"
        r"|\d+(?:\.\d+)?%?"
    )

    return re.findall(pattern, text.lower())


# ==================================================
# 3. GENERATE N-GRAMS
# ==================================================

def generate_ngrams(tokens, n):

    ngrams = []

    for i in range(len(tokens) - n + 1):

        gram = tuple(tokens[i:i + n])

        ngrams.append(gram)

    return ngrams


# ==================================================
# 4. ANALYZE ONE DOCUMENT
# ==================================================

def analyze_document(text):

    tokens = tokenize_text(text)

    document_result = {}

    for n in range(1, 6):

        ngrams = generate_ngrams(tokens, n)

        counter = Counter(ngrams)

        document_result[f"{n}-gram"] = {

            "total_occurrences": len(ngrams),

            "unique_ngrams": len(counter),

            "top_10": [

                {
                    "ngram": " ".join(gram),

                    "frequency": frequency

                }

                for gram, frequency in counter.most_common(10)

            ]

        }

    return document_result


# ==================================================
# 5. NORMALIZE CORPUS
# ==================================================

def normalize_corpus(corpus):

    if isinstance(corpus, dict):

        corpus = corpus.get("documents", [])

    if not isinstance(corpus, list):

        raise TypeError(
            "Expected a list of documents."
        )

    normalized = []

    for index, document in enumerate(corpus, start=1):

        if not isinstance(document, dict):

            raise TypeError(
                f"Document {index} is not a dictionary."
            )

        doc_id = (
            document.get("doc_id")
            or document.get("id")
            or document.get("document_id")
            or f"D{index:02d}"
        )

        name = (
            document.get("document_name")
            or document.get("name")
            or document.get("filename")
            or document.get("document")
            or doc_id
        )

        text = (
            document.get("cleaned_text")
            or document.get("cleaned")
            or document.get("text")
            or document.get("content")
            or document.get("original_text")
            or ""
        )

        if not isinstance(text, str) or not text.strip():

            raise ValueError(
                f"Missing text for {doc_id}. "
                f"Available fields: {list(document.keys())}"
            )

        normalized.append({

            "doc_id": str(doc_id),

            "document_name": str(name),

            "text": text

        })

    return normalized

    )


# --------------------------------------------------
# NLTK POS TAGGING
# --------------------------------------------------

def nltk_pos_tagging(text):

    tokens = tokenize_text(text)

    if not tokens:
        return []

    return [
        {
            "token": word,
            "pos": tag
        }
        for word, tag in pos_tag(tokens)
    ]


# --------------------------------------------------
# SPACY POS TAGGING
# --------------------------------------------------

def spacy_pos_tagging(text):

    doc = nlp(text)

    return [
        {
            "token": token.text,
            "pos": token.pos_,
            "tag": token.tag_
        }
        for token in doc
        if not token.is_space and not token.is_punct
    ]


# --------------------------------------------------
# POS TAGGING COMPARISON
# --------------------------------------------------

def compare_pos(nltk_results, spacy_results):

    nltk_tags = {}

    for item in nltk_results:
        token = item["token"].lower()
        nltk_tags.setdefault(token, []).append(item["pos"])

    spacy_tags = {}

    for item in spacy_results:
        token = item["token"].lower()
        spacy_tags.setdefault(token, []).append(item["pos"])

    common_words = set(nltk_tags) & set(spacy_tags)

    comparison = []

    for word in sorted(common_words):

        comparison.append({
            "word": word,
            "nltk_tags": nltk_tags[word],
            "spacy_tags": spacy_tags[word],
            "same_tag": (
                nltk_tags[word][0] == spacy_tags[word][0]
            )
        })

    return comparison

# ============================================================
# TOKEN EXTRACTION
# ============================================================

def extract_terms(query):

    tokens = re.findall(
        r"\b[a-zA-Z0-9]+(?:[-'][a-zA-Z0-9]+)*\b",
        query.lower()
    )

    return [
        token
        for token in tokens
        if token.upper() not in BOOLEAN_OPERATORS
    ]


def extract_keywords(terms):

    return [
        term
        for term in terms
        if term not in STOPWORDS
    ]


def extract_domain_terms(terms):

    return [
        term
        for term in terms
        if term in DOMAIN_TERMS
    ]


# ============================================================
# QUERY TYPE DETECTION
# ============================================================

def detect_query_type(query):

    # Explicit quoted phrase
    if re.search(r'"[^"]+"', query):
        return "PHRASE"

    # Boolean operators must be explicitly written
    # in uppercase to distinguish them from ordinary English.
    if re.search(r"\bNOT\b", query):
        return "NOT"

    if re.search(r"\bAND\b", query):
        return "AND"

    if re.search(r"\bOR\b", query):
        return "OR"

    return "KEYWORD"


# ============================================================
# QUERY COMPLEXITY
# ============================================================

def detect_complexity(terms, keywords, query_type):

    if query_type in {"AND", "OR", "NOT"}:
        return "HIGH"

    if len(keywords) <= 2:
        return "LOW"

    if len(keywords) <= 5:
        return "MEDIUM"

    return "HIGH"


# ============================================================
# QUERY UNDERSTANDING
# ============================================================

def understand_query(query):

    query = query.strip()

    if not query:
        raise ValueError("Query cannot be empty.")

    query_type = detect_query_type(query)

    terms = extract_terms(query)

    keywords = extract_keywords(terms)

    domain_terms = extract_domain_terms(terms)

    question_tokens = re.findall(
        r"\b[a-zA-Z]+\b",
        query.lower()
    )

    question_words_found = [
        word
        for word in question_tokens
        if word in QUESTION_WORDS
    ]

    complexity = detect_complexity(
        terms,
        keywords,
        query_type
    )

    return {
        "query": query,
        "query_type": query_type,
        "terms": terms,
        "keywords": keywords,
        "domain_terms": domain_terms,
        "term_count": len(terms),
        "keyword_count": len(keywords),
        "contains_question_word": bool(question_words_found),
        "question_words": question_words_found,
        "complexity": complexity
    }

# ==================================================
# TOKENIZATION

def tokenize(text):

    return re.findall(
        r"\b[a-zA-Z0-9]+(?:[-'][a-zA-Z0-9]+)*\b",
        str(text).lower()
    )


# ==================================================
# QUERY NORMALIZATION
# ==================================================

def remove_boolean_operators(text):

    return re.sub(
        r"\b(?:AND|OR|NOT)\b",
        " ",
        text,
        flags=re.IGNORECASE
    )


def get_query_terms(query, remove_stopwords=True):

    query = remove_boolean_operators(query)

    terms = tokenize(query)

    if remove_stopwords:

        terms = [
            term
            for term in terms
            if term not in STOPWORDS
        ]

    return terms


# ==================================================
# QUERY TERM EXPANSION
# ==================================================

def expand_query_term(term, index):

    term = term.lower().strip()

    if term == "covid":

        return ["covid-19", "covid"]

    return [term]


# ==================================================
# GET DOCUMENTS FOR A TERM
# ==================================================

def get_term_documents(term, index):

    matched_documents = set()

    for expanded_term in expand_query_term(
        term,
        index
    ):

        postings = index.get(expanded_term, {})

        if isinstance(postings, dict):

            matched_documents.update(
                postings.keys()
            )

    return matched_documents


# ==================================================
# TERM FREQUENCY
# ==================================================

def get_term_frequency(term, doc_id, index):

    frequency = 0

    for expanded_term in expand_query_term(
        term,
        index
    ):

        postings = index.get(expanded_term, {})

        if not isinstance(postings, dict):
            continue

        value = postings.get(doc_id, 0)

        if isinstance(value, dict):

            value = value.get(
                "frequency",
                value.get("tf", 0)
            )

        try:

            frequency += int(value)

        except (TypeError, ValueError):

            pass

    return frequency


# ==================================================
# KEYWORD SEARCH
# ==================================================

def keyword_search(query, index):

    terms = get_query_terms(query)

    matched_documents = set()

    for term in terms:

        matched_documents.update(
            get_term_documents(term, index)
        )

    return matched_documents


# ==================================================
# PHRASE SEARCH
# ==================================================

def phrase_search(query, corpus):

    phrase = query.strip().strip('"').lower()

    if not phrase:
        return set()

    phrase_tokens = tokenize(phrase)

    if not phrase_tokens:
        return set()

    normalized_phrase = " ".join(phrase_tokens)

    results = set()

    for doc_id, text in corpus.items():

        normalized_text = " ".join(
            tokenize(text)
        )

        if normalized_phrase in normalized_text:

            results.add(doc_id)

    return results


# ==================================================
# AND SEARCH
# ==================================================

def and_search(query, index):

    terms = get_query_terms(query)

    if not terms:
        return set()

    result = get_term_documents(
        terms[0],
        index
    )

    for term in terms[1:]:

        result = result.intersection(
            get_term_documents(term, index)
        )

    return result


# ==================================================
# OR SEARCH
# ==================================================

def or_search(query, index):

    terms = get_query_terms(query)

    result = set()

    for term in terms:

        result.update(
            get_term_documents(term, index)
        )

    return result


# ==================================================
# NOT SEARCH
# ==================================================

def not_search(query, index, all_documents):

    terms = get_query_terms(query)

    if not terms:
        return set()

    if len(terms) == 1:

        excluded = get_term_documents(
            terms[0],
            index
        )

        return all_documents - excluded

    positive_documents = get_term_documents(
        terms[0],
        index
    )

    excluded_documents = set()

    for term in terms[1:]:

        excluded_documents.update(
            get_term_documents(term, index)
        )

    return positive_documents - excluded_documents


# ==================================================
# IDF WEIGHT CALCULATION
# ==================================================

def calculate_idf(term, index):

    total_documents = len(
        index.get("documents", {})
    )

    if total_documents == 0:
        return 0.0

    document_frequency = len(
        get_term_documents(term, index)
    )

    idf = math.log(
        (total_documents + 1) /
        (document_frequency + 1)
    ) + 1

    return idf


# ==================================================
# DOCUMENT RANKING
# ==================================================

def rank_documents(doc_ids, query, index):

    terms = get_query_terms(query)

    unique_terms = list(dict.fromkeys(terms))

    ranked = []

    for doc_id in doc_ids:

        matched_terms = []

        total_frequency = 0

        weighted_score = 0.0

        for term in unique_terms:

            frequency = get_term_frequency(
                term,
                doc_id,
                index
            )

            if frequency <= 0:
                continue

            matched_terms.append(term)

            total_frequency += frequency

            idf = calculate_idf(
                term,
                index
            )

            tf_weight = 1 + math.log(frequency)

            weighted_score += (
                tf_weight * idf
            )

        coverage = (

            len(matched_terms) / len(unique_terms)

            if unique_terms

            else 0

        )

        score = (
            weighted_score
            + 2 * coverage
        )

        ranked.append({

            "document_id": doc_id,

            "score": round(score, 4),

            "matched_terms": sorted(matched_terms),

            "coverage": round(coverage, 4),

            "total_term_frequency": total_frequency,

            "weighted_score": round(
                weighted_score,
                4
            )

        })

    ranked.sort(

        key=lambda item: (
            -item["score"],
            item["document_id"]
        )

    )

    return ranked

def retrieve(query, method, index, corpus):

    method = method.upper().strip()

    all_documents = set(corpus.keys())

    if "index" in index and isinstance(
        index["index"], dict
    ):

        inverted_index = index["index"]

    else:

        inverted_index = index

    if method == "KEYWORD":

        results = keyword_search(
            query,
            inverted_index
        )

    elif method == "PHRASE":

        results = phrase_search(
            query,
            corpus
        )

    elif method == "AND":

        results = and_search(
            query,
            inverted_index
        )

    elif method == "OR":

        results = or_search(
            query,
            inverted_index
        )

    elif method == "NOT":

        results = not_search(
            query,
            inverted_index,
            all_documents
        )

    else:

        raise ValueError(
            f"Unsupported retrieval method: {method}"
        )

    ranked_results = rank_documents(
        results,
        query,
        inverted_index
    )

    return [
        item["document_id"]
        for item in ranked_results
    ]


# ==================================================
# DETAILED RETRIEVAL
# ==================================================

def retrieve_detailed(
    query,
    method,
    index,
    corpus
):

    retrieved_ids = retrieve(
        query,
        method,
        index,
        corpus
    )

    if "index" in index and isinstance(
        index["index"], dict
    ):

        inverted_index = index["index"]

    else:

        inverted_index = index

    ranked_results = rank_documents(
        retrieved_ids,
        query,
        inverted_index
    )

    return {

        "query": query,

        "method": method,

        "retrieved_count": len(ranked_results),

        "results": ranked_results

    }

# ==================================================

stemmer_methods = {
    "Porter": PorterStemmer(),
    "Lancaster": LancasterStemmer(),
    "Snowball": SnowballStemmer("english")
}


# ==================================================
# 3. TOKENIZATION
# ==================================================

def tokenize(text):

    return re.findall(
        r"\b[a-zA-Z0-9]+(?:-[a-zA-Z0-9]+)*\b",
        text.lower()
    )


# ==================================================
# 4. APPLY STEMMING
# ==================================================

def apply_stemming(tokens, stemmer):

    return [
        stemmer.stem(token)
        for token in tokens
    ]


# ==================================================
# 5. PROCESS CORPUS
# ==================================================

def process_corpus(documents):

    summary = {}
    document_results = []
    word_examples = {}

    for method_name, stemmer in stemmer_methods.items():

        all_stems = []
        total_tokens = 0

        for document in documents:

            tokens = tokenize(document["cleaned_text"])

            stems = apply_stemming(tokens, stemmer)

            total_tokens += len(stems)
            all_stems.extend(stems)

            document_results.append({
                "document_id": document["document_id"],
                "method": method_name,
                "token_count": len(stems),
                "vocabulary_size": len(set(stems)),
                "stemmed_tokens": stems
            })

            for original, stemmed in zip(tokens, stems):

                if original != stemmed:

                    word_examples.setdefault(
                        original, {}
                    )

                    word_examples[original][method_name] = stemmed

        summary[method_name] = {
            "total_tokens": total_tokens,
            "vocabulary_size": len(set(all_stems)),
            "average_tokens_per_document": round(
                total_tokens / len(documents), 2
            ) if documents else 0
        }

    return summary, document_results, word_examples

        )

def tokenize(text):

    )


# ==================================================

def remove_stopwords(tokens, stop_words):

    return [
        token
        for token in tokens
        if token.lower() not in stop_words
    ]

# 5. STATISTICS
# ==================================================

def calculate_statistics(tokens):

    return {
        "token_count": len(tokens),
        "vocabulary_size": len(set(tokens))
    }

# ==================================================

def process_documents(documents, stop_words):

    processed_documents = []
    comparison_results = []

    all_original_tokens = []
    all_filtered_tokens = []

    for document in documents:

        document_id = document["document_id"]

        text = document["cleaned_text"]

        original_tokens = tokenize(text)

        filtered_tokens = remove_stopwords(
            original_tokens,
            stop_words
        )

        all_original_tokens.extend(original_tokens)
        all_filtered_tokens.extend(filtered_tokens)

        original_stats = calculate_statistics(
            original_tokens
        )

        filtered_stats = calculate_statistics(
            filtered_tokens
        )

        removed_count = (
            len(original_tokens) - len(filtered_tokens)
        )

        removal_percentage = (
            removed_count / len(original_tokens) * 100
            if original_tokens else 0
        )

        processed_documents.append({
            "document_id": document_id,
            "file_name": document["file_name"],
            "tokens_before_removal": original_tokens,
            "tokens_after_removal": filtered_tokens,
            "token_count_before": original_stats["token_count"],
            "token_count_after": filtered_stats["token_count"],
            "vocabulary_before": original_stats["vocabulary_size"],
            "vocabulary_after": filtered_stats["vocabulary_size"]
        })

        comparison_results.append({
            "document_id": document_id,
            "token_count_before": original_stats["token_count"],
            "token_count_after": filtered_stats["token_count"],
            "removed_tokens": removed_count,
            "removal_percentage": round(
                removal_percentage, 2
            ),
            "vocabulary_before": original_stats["vocabulary_size"],
            "vocabulary_after": filtered_stats["vocabulary_size"]
        })

    corpus_summary = {
        "total_tokens_before": len(all_original_tokens),
        "total_tokens_after": len(all_filtered_tokens),
        "total_removed_tokens": (
            len(all_original_tokens) - len(all_filtered_tokens)
        ),
        "vocabulary_before": len(set(all_original_tokens)),
        "vocabulary_after": len(set(all_filtered_tokens))
    }

    corpus_summary["removal_percentage"] = round(
        corpus_summary["total_removed_tokens"]
        / corpus_summary["total_tokens_before"] * 100,
        2
    ) if corpus_summary["total_tokens_before"] else 0

    return (
        processed_documents,
        comparison_results,
        corpus_summary
    )

def clean_text(text):

    original_text = text

    # Convert HTML entities and remove HTML tags
    text = re.sub(r"&amp;", "and", text)
    text = re.sub(r"&lt;", " less than ", text)
    text = re.sub(r"&gt;", " greater than ", text)

    text = re.sub(r"<[^>]+>", " ", text)

    # Remove URLs
    text = re.sub(
        r"https?://\S+|www\.\S+",
        " ",
        text
    )

    # Normalize Unicode dash characters
    text = re.sub(r"[–—−]", "-", text)

    # Lowercase text
    text = text.lower()

    # Preserve letters, digits, spaces, and hyphens
    text = re.sub(
        r"[^a-z0-9\s-]",
        " ",
        text
    )

    # Remove standalone hyphens
    text = re.sub(r"(?<!\w)-|-(?!\w)", " ", text)

    # Normalize repeated whitespace
    text = re.sub(r"\s+", " ", text).strip()

    return {
        "cleaned_text": text,
        "original_character_count": len(original_text),
        "cleaned_character_count": len(text)
    }


# ==================================================
# 3. PROCESS CORPUS
# ==================================================

def preprocess_documents(documents):

    cleaned_documents = []

    for document in documents:

        result = clean_text(document["text"])

        cleaned_document = {
            "document_id": document["document_id"],
            "file_name": document["file_name"],
            "document_name": document["document_name"],
            "original_text": document["text"],
            "cleaned_text": result["cleaned_text"],
            "original_character_count":
                result["original_character_count"],
            "cleaned_character_count":
                result["cleaned_character_count"]
        }

        cleaned_documents.append(cleaned_document)

    return cleaned_documents

def save_cleaned_corpus(documents):

    RESULT_DIR.mkdir(

from pathlib import Path
import json
import re
import csv

from nltk.tokenize import TreebankWordTokenizer
import spacy


# ==================================================
# 1. PATHS
# ==================================================

PROJECT_ROOT = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[1]

RESULT_DIR = PROJECT_ROOT / "results"

INPUT_FILE = RESULT_DIR / "cleaned_corpus.json"

OUTPUT_JSON = RESULT_DIR / "tokenization_comparison.json"
OUTPUT_CSV = RESULT_DIR / "tokenization_comparison.csv"

nlp = spacy.load("en_core_web_sm")

nltk_tokenizer = TreebankWordTokenizer()


# ==================================================
# 2. CUSTOM HEALTHCARE TOKENIZER
# ==================================================

def custom_tokenize(text):

    pattern = (
        r"COVID-19"
        r"|SARS-CoV-2"
        r"|MERS-CoV"
        r"|H1N1"
        r"|PCR"
        r"|RT-PCR"
        r"|[a-zA-Z]+(?:-[a-zA-Z0-9]+)*"
        r"|\d+(?:\.\d+)?%?"
    )

    return re.findall(pattern, text, flags=re.IGNORECASE)


# ==================================================
# 3. TOKENIZATION METHODS
# ==================================================

def nltk_tokenize(text):

    return nltk_tokenizer.tokenize(text)


def spacy_tokenize(text):

    doc = nlp(text)

    return [
        token.text
        for token in doc
        if not token.is_space
    ]


def hybrid_tokenize(text):

    # Use custom rules for healthcare terms,
    # followed by spaCy tokenization for ordinary text.

    domain_terms = [
        "COVID-19",
        "SARS-CoV-2",
        "MERS-CoV",
        "H1N1",
        "RT-PCR"
    ]

    placeholders = {}

    protected_text = text

    for index, term in enumerate(domain_terms):

        placeholder = f"DOMAINTERM{index}TOKEN"

        pattern = re.compile(
            re.escape(term),
            flags=re.IGNORECASE
        )

        if pattern.search(protected_text):

            protected_text = pattern.sub(
                placeholder,
                protected_text
            )

            placeholders[placeholder.lower()] = term

    tokens = spacy_tokenize(protected_text)

    final_tokens = []

    for token in tokens:

        key = token.lower()

        if key in placeholders:
            final_tokens.append(placeholders[key])
        else:
            final_tokens.append(token)

    return final_tokens


# ==================================================
# 4. VOCABULARY STATISTICS
# ==================================================

def calculate_statistics(token_lists):

    all_tokens = [
        token.lower()
        for tokens in token_lists
        for token in tokens
    ]

    total_tokens = len(all_tokens)

    vocabulary = set(all_tokens)

    document_count = len(token_lists)

    average_tokens = (
        total_tokens / document_count
        if document_count else 0
    )

    return {
        "total_tokens": total_tokens,
        "vocabulary_size": len(vocabulary),
        "average_tokens_per_document": round(
            average_tokens, 2
        )
    }


# ==================================================
# 5. PROCESS CORPUS
# ==================================================

def run_comparison(documents):

    methods = {
        "NLTK": nltk_tokenize,
        "spaCy": spacy_tokenize,
        "Custom": custom_tokenize,
        "Hybrid": hybrid_tokenize
    }

    results = {}
    document_results = []

    for method_name, tokenizer in methods.items():

        token_lists = []

        for document in documents:

            text = document["cleaned_text"]

            tokens = tokenizer(text)

            token_lists.append(tokens)

            document_results.append({
                "document_id": document["document_id"],
                "method": method_name,
                "token_count": len(tokens),
                "tokens": tokens
            })

        results[method_name] = calculate_statistics(
            token_lists
        )

    return results, document_results


# ==================================================
# 6. SAVE RESULTS
# ==================================================

def save_results(summary, document_results):

    with open(
        OUTPUT_JSON,
        "w",
        encoding="utf-8"
    ) as file:

        json.dump(
            {
                "summary": summary,
                "document_results": document_results
            },
            file,
            indent=4,
            ensure_ascii=False
        )

    with open(
        OUTPUT_CSV,
        "w",
        newline="",
        encoding="utf-8"
    ) as file:

        writer = csv.DictWriter(
            file,
            fieldnames=[
                "document_id",
                "method",
                "token_count",
                "tokens"
            ]
        )

        writer.writeheader()

        for row in document_results:

            writer.writerow({
                **row,
                "tokens": " ".join(row["tokens"])
            })

def main():

    print("=" * 65)
